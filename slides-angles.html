Money at the moment of crisis

Why this angleFamilies pay 63% of health costs themselves, often in the middle of an emergency (reading p.7).
What it could look like
  • Find out what families still pay for a birth: transport, medicines, supplies, informal fees
  • Ideas to explore: transport vouchers, a community emergency fund, free newborn care packages
  • Lead to research: Sierra Leone made care free for pregnant women and young children in 2010
Push it further
  • Who pays when the donor leaves?
  • Could free care make clinics more crowded and worse?

Teen mothers

Why this angle1 in 4 women aged 20–24 married before 18; UNICEF links Liberia's high newborn deaths to teen pregnancy (p.5, 9).
What it could look like
  • Adolescent-friendly care: private, respectful, no judgement
  • Peer mothers who walk with a pregnant girl to check-ups
  • Keep girls in school: a longer-term newborn intervention
Push it further
  • How do you reach a girl whose husband or in-laws decide?
  • What would a 16-year-old need to feel safe at a clinic?

Beating the rainy season

Why this angleSix months of rain turn roads to mud; late referral and delayed ambulances are common factors in newborn deaths (p.3, 9).
What it could look like
  • Maternity waiting homes near clinics, filled before May
  • Motorbike or boat transport schemes run by the community
  • Pre-position supplies before the rains; a phone line for referral
Push it further
  • What does the trip look like in August versus February?
  • Who drives at 2 a.m., and who pays them?

ANGLE 4 OF 85 S's: Staff · Stuff · Space

Quality inside the clinic

Why this angle4 in 5 births already happen in a facility, yet newborn deaths stay high (p.9). Facilities lack staff, supplies and incubators.
What it could look like
  • Monthly skills drills for every birth attendant (breathing, warmth, infection)
  • A simple newborn corner: warm surface, bag-and-mask, clean water, power
  • Respectful care, so women want to come back
Push it further
  • How do skills stay sharp when births are rare in a small clinic?
  • What is the cheapest thing that saves the most babies on day one?

ANGLE 5 OF 85 S's: Staff · Systems · Social support

Community health workers & midwives

Why this angleCHAs reach homes 5 km+ away; Trained Traditional Midwives are deeply trusted (p.13).
What it could look like
  • Pair a CHA with a TTM for birth preparation and home visits in week 1
  • Danger-sign checklists in pictures, for families who can't read
  • Reliable pay, supervision and supplies, or none of it works (p.15)
Push it further
  • What would make a TTM want to bring women to the clinic?
  • Why does the gender of the health worker matter here?

ANGLE 6 OF 85 S's: Systems

Learning from every death

Why this angleWithout data, no one can see where babies are lost. Ebola and COVID showed how fast care can collapse (p.6).
What it could look like
  • Register every birth and every newborn death in the pilot area
  • Review each death with staff and the community: which delay? what fix?
  • Simple monthly dashboard the county team actually uses
Push it further
  • Who acts on the data, and how fast?
  • How do you review deaths without blaming one nurse?

ANGLE 7 OF 85 S's: Space · Social support

Small babies, warm hands

Why this anglePrematurity and low birth weight are leading causes; incubators are scarce (p.9–10). PIH-Liberia already runs Kangaroo Mother Care (p.12).
What it could look like
  • Skin-to-skin care from the first hour, at the clinic and at home
  • Support for breastfeeding and follow-up visits
  • Involve fathers and grandmothers so the mother can rest
Push it further
  • Why build on an existing program instead of starting something new?
  • What does a mother need to keep skin-to-skin going for days?

ANGLE 8 OF 85 S's: Social support

Power, gender & decisions

Why this angle41% of partnered women lack full say over sex and contraception; husbands and elders often decide when to seek care (p.4–5).
What it could look like
  • Birth-planning talks that include husbands and mothers-in-law
  • Women's groups that plan transport and savings together
  • Community leaders who publicly back early care-seeking
Push it further
  • How do you shift a norm without blaming a culture?
  • Which change would show up fastest in newborn survival?
