The gap

How many newborns die per 1,000 live births in Liberia, and in Canada?

About 33 in Liberia (2024) and 3.3 in Canada: roughly ten times higher. (reading p.8)

What counts as a 'neonatal' death?

A death in the first 28 days of life. (p.1)

When do most newborn deaths happen?

About 70% happen in the first week (days 0–6), many in the first 24 hours. (p.8)

Has Liberia's rate always been stuck?

No. It fell from about 61 per 1,000 in 1990 to about 35 in 2010, then barely moved (about 33 in 2024). (UN IGME)

Is Liberia the worst in the world?

No. It ranked about 7th highest of 200 countries in 2024; South Sudan, Nigeria and Pakistan were higher. (UN IGME)

Why babies die

Name four direct (proximal) causes.

Prematurity and low birth weight, birth asphyxia, infections and sepsis, and complications in labour. (p.9)

What are the three delays?

1 Deciding to seek care · 2 Reaching care in time · 3 Receiving good care on arrival. (p.10–11)

What share of births now happen in a health facility?

About 4 in 5 (2019–20), up from 37% in 2007. Deaths fell far less, which points to quality of care. (p.9; DHS)

Give three distal (underlying) causes.

Poverty, little schooling, gender inequality, geography, unsafe water and smoke, a weak health system. (p.11–12)

How does violence against women affect newborns?

It is linked to preterm birth and low birth weight, which raise the risk of newborn death. (p.5, 12)

Place, history, money

When is the rainy season, and why does it matter?

May to October. Unpaved roads turn to mud and floods cut communities off, delaying care. (p.3)

Who founded the settlement that became Liberia?

The American Colonization Society (founded 1816); freed African Americans arrived from 1822. (p.3–4)

What did the civil wars do to health care?

About 242 of 293 public facilities were destroyed, and fewer places remained to train health workers. (p.6–7)

What happened to maternal care during Ebola?

First pregnancy visits fell 43% and facility births 38%; 189 health workers died. (p.6)

Who pays for health care in Liberia?

Families pay about 63% out of pocket; donors provided 52% of the public health budget in 2021. (p.7)

What changed in 2025?

U.S. aid fell about 35% and about US$434 million of planned funding was cancelled. (p.8)

People & programs

What do Community Health Assistants (CHAs) do?

Trained locals who serve homes 5 km or more from a facility, supervised by CHSSs. (p.13)

Who are Trained Traditional Midwives?

Trusted community birth helpers who educate, prepare families for birth, spot danger signs and refer women to facilities. (p.13)

What are PIH's 5 S's?

Staff, stuff, space, systems and social support. (p.12)

Where does Partners In Health work in Liberia?

Maryland County in the rural southeast, including J.J. Dossen Hospital. (p.12)

What is Kangaroo Mother Care?

Skin-to-skin care and breastfeeding support for small and preterm babies; PIH-Liberia runs a program. (p.12)
