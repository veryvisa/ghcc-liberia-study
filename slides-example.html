OPENING≈ 20 sec

First Day, Every Baby

"A baby born in Liberia today is ten times more likely to die in its first month than a baby born in Canada. Most of those deaths happen in the first week, many on the very first day."
Our idea in one line
Make the first 24 hours safe for every newborn in Maryland County, using people and programs that already exist.
Why it matters
Liberia's newborn survival has barely improved since 2010, while its neighbours kept improving.

ROOT CAUSE & FOCUS≈ 50 sec

The weak link is the moment of birth and the days after

Delay 1 · Decide
Families may not see danger signs; women often need permission and money to go (p.4–5, 10).
Delay 2 · Reach
Mud roads and floods for six months; a sick baby may need a second trip to J.J. Dossen (p.3, 10–12).
Delay 3 · Receive ★
4 in 5 births are already in facilities, yet deaths stay high: too few trained staff, supplies and incubators (p.9, 11).
Our focus: the first 24 hours (Delay 3), plus getting small or sick babies to help in the first week (Delays 1 and 2). That is where most deaths happen, and where facility births have not yet paid off.

OUR SOLUTION≈ 90 sec

Three parts, built on what Liberia already has

A · Ready at birth
Every birth attendant at pilot clinics trained and mentored monthly in newborn resuscitation (e.g. the Helping Babies Breathe course) and warmth; a simple kit: bag-and-mask, warm wraps, clean cord care.
B · Warm hands
Skin-to-skin Kangaroo Mother Care starting in the first hour for small babies, as WHO recommends, then continued at home. It needs no electricity and expands PIH-Liberia's existing program (p.12).
C · Never alone in week 1
A community health worker and a Trained Traditional Midwife visit on days 1, 3 and 7 with a danger-sign checklist; phone + motorbike referral; waiting homes near clinics before the rains.
Staffmentored midwives; CHAs, CHPs, TTMs
Stuffresuscitation & warmth kits
SpaceKMC corner; waiting homes
Systemsreferral, rainy-season plan, death reviews
Social supporthome visits; families & elders involved

  • Is three parts too many? What would you cut first?
  • What could make families trust and use part C?

HOW IT WORKS≈ 50 sec

Hawa's first week, today and with our plan

Hawa (invented for this example) is 17 and pregnant with her first child. She lives 12 km from the nearest clinic in Maryland County, and it is the rainy season.
Late pregnancy

Today: stays home until labour starts; the road floods

With our plan: moves to a waiting home near the clinic two weeks early

Birth

Today: the baby doesn't breathe; staff unsure what to do

With our plan: a trained midwife uses bag-and-mask in the first minute

First hours

Today: small baby gets cold

With our plan: skin-to-skin on Hawa's chest, breastfeeding within an hour

Days 1–3

Today: home; nobody checks

With our plan: CHA + TTM visit; check warmth, feeding, cord

Day 5

Today: fever; family unsure, no money to travel

With our plan: CHA spots the danger sign; motorbike to the clinic

ROLLOUT & COST≈ 50 sec

Start small, learn fast, scale through the national system

Year 1 · Pilot
A few clinics in Maryland County and their communities, with PIH-Liberia and the county health team.
Year 2 · Learn
Review every newborn death, fix what doesn't work, write a simple how-to guide.
Year 3+ · Scale
Spread to other southeastern counties through the Community Health Program Policy 2025–2035.
Main costs
training and mentoring, kits, motorbike fuel, running waiting homes. [ your team estimates ]
Who pays
start-up from a partner grant; then line items in county and national budgets.
What lasts
skills stay with staff; workers are part of the national program, not a project.
